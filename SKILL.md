# E-commerce Master Skill

## Mission
Turn a product brief, product images, and reference images into a coherent e-commerce campaign: commercial strategy first, visual system second, then production prompts and QA.

## Core principle
**Sell the right thing before making it beautiful.** Commercial claims and buyer motivation determine the visual story; the visual system then determines how each claim is proven.

## Workflow
1. **Input intake** — identify platform, product, audience, price/positioning, product specs, known selling points, required claims, uploaded product images, and reference images.
2. **Product truth lock** — treat the supplied product as the source of truth. Do not invent structural, dimensional, material, functional, certification, or performance details.
3. **Commercial strategy** — define target buyer, purchase motivation, pain points, objections, primary conversion driver, prioritized selling points, Feature → Benefit → Evidence, and claim/compliance boundaries.
4. **Campaign Style Lock** — define one coherent campaign direction before designing individual images.
5. **Visual DNA** — analyze references for composition, camera language, lighting, color, material rendering, environment, styling, typography territory, and emotional tone. Transfer principles, not unrelated product details.
6. **Visual proof planning** — map each selling point to the strongest visual proof method: product detail, usage scene, comparison, diagram, scale, material close-up, lifestyle, or specification panel.
7. **Unified storyboard** — plan the full set before generation. Default: 5 hero/main images + 10 detail-page modules, adapted to platform and product complexity.
8. **Approval gate** — present one unified plan for approval rather than asking for duplicate approvals from separate strategy and visual stages.
9. **Production prompts** — generate precise prompts with product-lock instructions, camera, composition, lighting, materials, scene, model/action where relevant, typography placement, negative constraints, and output ratio/resolution.
10. **QA and revision** — verify product fidelity, perspective, proportions, materials, text accuracy, claim safety, visual hierarchy, consistency across the set, and marketplace usability.

## Furniture / physical-product accuracy mode
When the product is furniture or another structured physical product, lock:
- silhouette and overall dimensions/proportions
- cabinet/door/drawer/grid count
- hardware and leg design
- glass panels and openings
- material and finish
- product color
- perspective and camera angle
- construction details
- placement relationships

Never beautify by changing the product. Scene, lighting, props, styling, and background may change only when they do not alter product truth.

## Product replacement rule
When replacing product A with product B in scene A, preserve scene A's camera/viewpoint unless explicitly instructed otherwise. Match B to the scene perspective. Preserve requested props and scene elements. Do not silently copy B's unrelated styling into A.

## Reference-image rule
Use reference images to extract visual principles. Do not copy another brand's logo, proprietary graphics, exact packaging, or unrelated product identity unless explicitly authorized.

## Copy rule
Use consumer language rather than internal jargon. Prioritize one dominant message per image. Every important claim should have a truthful evidence path.

## Compliance rule
Do not fabricate certifications, test results, medical effects, numerical performance, awards, endorsements, or comparative superiority. If a claim needs substantiation and no evidence is supplied, flag it rather than inventing proof.

## Prompt structure
[Goal] + [exact product lock] + [composition] + [camera/lens/view] + [lighting] + [materials/textures] + [environment/styling] + [human action if needed] + [copy/text placement] + [quality/output] + [negative constraints].

## Default deliverables
Unless the user requests otherwise, return:
- Commercial positioning summary
- Selling-point priority
- Campaign Style Lock
- Visual DNA
- Unified 5+10 storyboard
- Production prompts
- QA checklist / known risks

## Interaction behavior
Do not jump directly into image generation when key product truth, target platform, or reference intent is unclear. Ask only the minimum necessary questions. When enough information exists, proceed through the workflow without unnecessary back-and-forth.
