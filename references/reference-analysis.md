# Reference Analysis

## Classify inputs
- Product image: source of product appearance, proportions, materials, colors, visible labels and structure.
- First-head reference: marketplace layout, scan path, hierarchy and commercial structure.
- Detail-page reference: vertical narrative, proof modules, density and sequencing.
- Poster/mood reference: camera attitude, lighting, typography, emotional temperature and creative method.
- Color/mood reference: color relationships and atmosphere, not permission to copy exact hues.

## Reference is language, not scene
Inherit light behavior, typography attitude, density rhythm, commercial temperature and module rhythm. Do not automatically inherit specific surfaces, locations, props, backgrounds, brands, people, copy, claims or prices. Any single reference-scene element is capped at 30% of a set unless the user explicitly requests a scene lock.

## Flavor contract
Record:
- commercial genre
- camera attitude
- human-product relationship
- spatial tension
- typography behavior
- emotional temperature
- material/light behavior
- information density

Then state what must carry, what can adapt, what must not copy, and what would cause flavor loss.

## Product-use plausibility
Before using a reference action, identify how a real person naturally holds, wears, carries, opens, applies, stores or operates the target product. Transfer the intent of the reference pose, not an awkward literal pose.

## Model source
When people are required, determine whether to use a reference-image model, product-image model, or generated model. For recurring models, lock apparent age range, face structure, hair, skin tone, body type, expression range and styling attitude. Vary poses according to the message while preserving identity.

## Claim safety
Classify each point as visible, category-generic, user-provided, or unsupported. Use only the first three. Unsupported ratings, certifications, medical/health results, exact performance, prices or official claims must not be invented.

## Taste-gap audit
After generation, compare outputs against the flavor contract rather than only color/layout. Check camera attitude, product-human plausibility, information density, reference-scene over-copying, visual diversity, Picture Solo success, icon leakage, and model consistency.
