# Visual Proof Grammar

## Planning order
1. Define one core message.
2. Identify buyer anxiety/desire.
3. Ask what picture proves it fastest with copy hidden.
4. Select the main subject.
5. Decide whether the product must appear.
6. Select scene, macro, mechanism, comparison, parameter or lifestyle proof.
7. Apply reference DNA without overriding proof.
8. Remove anything that distracts from proof.

## Picture Solo Test
Before every prompt write:
> The picture is [concrete scene]. With all text hidden, a buyer looking for 2 seconds would understand [specific message].

If the statement cannot be true without the headline, redesign the picture.

## Product-presence rule
- Hero/closing: full product is appropriate.
- Operation: medium product in use.
- Detail/craft/parameter: partial crop or component.
- Scene-fit/lifestyle: small context product or none.
- Material/texture, sensory result, ingredient, mechanism: usually no packaging.

The product is not required to dominate every screen.

## Consumer voice
Copy should tell the buyer what they get, feel or stop worrying about. Avoid manufacturer/spec-sheet language such as "采用XX技术", "本产品具有XX功能", "符合XX标准" and empty superlatives. Apply the "so what?" test to every headline.

## No-icon rule
No icons, pictograms, checkmark rows, ingredient bubbles, badge ribbons or decorative feature-icon systems. The main visual must carry the proof. Text labels are allowed only when they identify real visible parts or essential factual annotations.

## Density rhythm
Mix light, medium and heavy screens. Heavy density is reserved for mechanism, comparison, parameter or trust proof. Do not make every screen a card grid.
